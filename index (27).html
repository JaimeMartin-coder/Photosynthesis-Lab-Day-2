Spinach Disk Photosynthesis Lab

How does the distance of a lamp change how fast spinach disks float?

Background and directions

Performance expectationHS-LS1-5 Science practicePlanning and carrying out investigations; analyzing data Core ideaLS1.C Organization for matter and energy flow Crosscutting conceptEnergy and matter

Small disks are punched out of a spinach leaf and placed in a baking soda solution. The disks sink at first because their air spaces are filled with liquid. When the leaf photosynthesizes, it makes oxygen gas. The gas fills the air spaces and the disks float to the top. The faster photosynthesis happens, the sooner the disks float.

Directions

  1. You will run 3 trials in this order: 25 cm (control), then 15 cm, then 35 cm. Each trial uses 10 fresh disks and lasts 10 minutes.
  2. For each trial, drag the clamp lamp to the correct distance from the cup. You can also use the move buttons.
  3. Press Start trial and watch the disks.
  4. The lab runs in real time. It cannot be sped up, slowed down, or paused. A beep sounds at the end of every minute. Count the disks that are floating and write the number on your own data table. Count a disk only when it reaches the surface.
  5. If all 10 disks reach the surface before 10 minutes, press Stop trial. Write down the time on the clock. That is how long it took for all 10 to float.
  6. After all 3 trials, finish your graph and conclusion on your paper.

Each trial takes 10 full minutes, so have your data table ready before you press Start. Turn your sound on so you can hear the minute beep.
